Your radar is already tracking. This is the step that centers it on you — about thirty seconds, and it stays set.

It works out roughly where it is from your internet connection. That's usually close, but it can land on your provider's equipment in another town, which is how a radar ends up showing a different city's aircraft. Setting your exact position here replaces that guess for good.

Device address

Your radar's address on your home network. It only works from a phone or computer on that same WiFi.

Where do I find it?

If you've just connected your radar to WiFi, it's on the screen in front of you, under step 2.

Otherwise:

  1. Tap the gear icon at the bottom-left of the radar screen.
  2. Tap Info & Credits.
  3. Read the number next to Device.

Your location

The center of the radar. Letting your browser supply it is accurate to a few meters and takes one tap.

or type it
Check that minus sign. A positive longitude puts your radar in central Asia, where it will find no aircraft at all. Across the Americas this number starts with a minus.
How do I look these up?
  1. Open Google Maps and find your home.
  2. Press and hold on it (or right-click on a computer).
  3. Two numbers appear. Copy the first into Latitude and the second into Longitude.
  4. Across the Americas the second number is negative. Keep the minus sign.

Weather station

Conditions come from the nearest airport that reports them, which the radar finds on its own once it knows where you are. Pick your own if you'd rather have a particular one.

What's a station code?

Four letters identifying an airport that publishes weather readings. Your nearest large airport almost always does.

In the United States they start with K — KMHT is Manchester, KCON is Concord, KSEA is Seattle. Canada starts with C, Alaska and Hawaii with P. If you're unsure, leave this on "Nearest to me" and the radar will work it out.

Search radius

How far out to look. Smaller shows fewer, closer aircraft on a less crowded screen.

Moving to a different WiFi network?

This disconnects the radar and restarts it in setup mode, so you can join it to another network the same way you did the first time. Your location, station and radius are kept.
